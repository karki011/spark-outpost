# Harness overhead · 2026-10-01

Where Spark's extra cost and time go on the [story benchmark](story-2026-10-01.md), what was trimmed, and what it bought. Branch `chore/spark-overhead` (from `checkpoint/harness-m6-round3`), policy `overhead-1-spec-plan-rereview`. The raw data is in [overhead-2026-10-01.json](overhead-2026-10-01.json): every run, every agent turn with its model, effort, tokens, cost and timing, before and after.

**Spend: $1.96 of the $8 cap** (the re-run of arm a and direct Claude, n = 2 each). The attribution itself cost nothing: it reads the transcripts the story runs left behind.

## Headline

- **Before, 72% of arm a's cost per pass was the developer, not the models.** Arm a spent $1.07 a run on models, and $2.78 a pass on 5 developer answers across 3 runs. Across all 16 harness runs at Automatic, 16 of the 23 answers were the "Build this plan?" card, 6 were keep cards and 1 was a budget card. Direct Claude asked nothing.
- **The model spend split three ways:** the plan-first turn ($0.36), the Builder ($0.35) and the Critic loop ($0.32). **The wall time was mostly the Critic:** 370 of 674 s. A Codex Critic at high effort took 138 s per review on average (160–200 s under load), and arm a ran 2–3 reviews a run.
- **The plan turn fed the Critic loop.** The story is already a spec (7 bullets, each naming files and functions). Spark still wrote its own 13–47-step plan, and the Critic then checked the build against those sub-steps. In 2 of the 3 arm-a runs the fix round was triggered by a step the plan turn invented ("the agreed test case for a fresh database", "validate in the API layer first"), not by anything in the request. Slow re-reviews then caused keep cards: one re-review failed, and one ran past its fix round's 4-minute budget.
- **Two trims are in** (details below): a request that is already a spec becomes the agreed plan with no plan turn and no card, and a re-review runs at medium effort. **After the trims, arm a passed 2/2 at $0.61 a pass and 185 s, down from $3.84 and 674 s.** Direct Claude in the same session: 2/2 at $0.37 and 116 s. The gap went from 9.6× to 1.6× on cost per pass. Part of the wall-time drop is lower machine load (see Limits).

## Method

- **Sources.** The story runs left their agent transcripts on disk. For Claude, that is `~/.claude/projects/<run worktree>/*.jsonl`, with subagents under `<session>/subagents/`. For Codex, it is `~/.codex/sessions/…/rollout-*.jsonl`, matched to a run by `cwd`. Each file gives every model call's usage (input, cache read, cache write with its TTL, output, reasoning) and timestamps.
- **Cost.** Claude cost is recomputed from the model-card prices, with 1-hour cache writes at 2× input. That is how the Claude CLI bills them, and it matches the CLI's own `cost-state` to the cent. Codex cost comes from token counts at card prices, and it matches Spark's recorded Critic costs. The small remainder against the recorded run cost is Jev plus the CLI's Haiku title calls.
- **Turns.** Each turn is labelled by its prompt: plan-first (`Before anything is built, write the plan`), Explore subagent, coordinator, Builder, Builder fix (a resumed turn in the Builder's session), piece Builder, integrator, second scout, Critic and re-review.
- **Wall.** Wall time per phase is the union of that phase's turn spans. "Orchestration" is the wall time outside every agent turn: session spawns, Jev calls, checks, cards, git and snapshots.
- **Cost per pass.** This follows the story benchmark: (spend + developer time at $100/h) ÷ passes. Developer time is 1 min per answered card and 30 min per failed story.

## Top drivers (arm a, before)

1. **The "Build this plan?" card at Automatic: $1.67 per run, 43% of cost per pass.** It is 16 of the 23 answers in the harness runs. At Automatic the card waits for the developer; only Full auto counts down.
2. **Keep cards after a Critic loop that did not finish cleanly: $1.11 per run (29%).** a-pilot: the re-review failed after 7 s, so the verdict was unclear and a keep card followed. a-2: a 166 s high-effort re-review pushed fix round 1 past its 4-minute budget, so the round was cut and a keep card followed. In d, all 3 keep cards came from the Codex Critic failing behind the 429 proxy.
3. **The Critic loop's wall time: 370 s of 674 s (55%), $0.32.** It is slow because of Codex high effort per call (33 s per call, 2K reasoning tokens) and because of repeats: 3 of 3 arm-a runs had "concerns". The findings were plan sub-steps in 2 runs, and in 1 a percent-rounding choice that the hidden tests don't pin (another run was told to use `Math.round`, a third switched to `BigInt`). None of them would have failed a hidden test.
4. **The plan-first turn: $0.36 and 127 s per run (33% of spend).** The Explore subagent is $0.14 and 77 s of that, used in 14 of 16 plan turns. The subagent exists because the Spark team line gives every tooled Claude session the Agent tool and tells it to delegate.
5. **Structural: many short sessions, each writing its own cache.** Cache writes are 47% of all harness model spend ($8.98 of $19.23 across the 16 runs), and the 1-hour TTL premium alone is $3.02 (16%). Every Claude session writes its prompt prefix at the 1-hour price. Direct Claude writes one prefix and then reads it: 659K cached reads against 43K written. In the coordinated arms, the coordinator adds $0.29 and 57 s per run and engaged in 13 of 13, but its piece list was discarded in 10 of 16 (B3/B4 in the story report).

**Same pattern on the dev day ([e2e-2026-09-29](e2e-2026-09-29.md)).** The harness at Review each step and direct Claude spent almost the same ($2.52 vs $2.62 for 14 sessions). Cost per pass was $1.25 against $0.54 only because of answers (9 against 3): 5 keep cards, 2 risky-change cards and 1 plan card, all by design at Review each step. The spec trim below does not apply at that level.

## What changed

| # | Change | Where | Level | Tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **A request that is already a spec is the agreed plan.** Three conditions: 3+ listed items, at least ⅔ of them naming a file or code (a path or a `backticked` identifier), and no question outside code. When they hold, the request is saved as the approved plan (source "saved by you", option `request_is_plan`) and the Builder starts at once: no plan turn, no card, and the work log says "Your request is already a plan (…)". Everything downstream is unchanged. The Builder and the Critic see it as `## Agreed plan`. The coordinator still engages on its open items, so the parallel default is untouched. Full auto still puts "Plan it followed" in the PR body. | `builder-complexity.ts` `specByRule`, `plan-first.ts` `requestIsThePlan` / `adoptRequestAsPlan` | Automatic and Full auto. Review each step keeps the plan turn and the card. | `builder-complexity.test.ts`, `plan-first.test.ts`, `test/orchestration-spec-plan.db.test.ts` (Automatic: no card, the plan row, the Builder prompt; Review each step: still plans first) |
| 2 | **Re-reviews run at medium effort.** The Critic that checks a fix round is the same provider and model at `CRITIC_REREVIEW_EFFORT` (`medium`, when the provider offers it). The first review stays at high effort. | `routing.ts` `rereviewCritic`, `member-routes.ts`, `tiers.ts` `loweredEffort`, `advisor/policy.ts` | All levels | `routing.test.ts` |

`POLICY_VERSION` is now `overhead-1-spec-plan-rereview`, and HARNESS_PLAN.md (the plan-first and Critic fix-round sections, and Audit) describes both changes. `pnpm check` and the full `pnpm test:db` pass (117 files, 673 tests).

Why these two are low risk:

- **(1)** Nothing a user writes as a spec gets less than before. Their own bullets become the plan the Builder follows and the Critic checks. What goes away is a second, model-written plan that the user rubber-stamped with one click, whose invented sub-steps the Critic then enforced. The evidence says the plan turn does not raise pass rates. Direct Claude never plans and passed 3/3, and now 2/2. Round 10 measured planned turns at 2.1–4.4× cost with no pass-rate gain. On the dev day, a big request that missed plan-first (B4) passed on its first build.
- **(2)** A re-review is told which findings to check, and the checks run again anyway. In the re-run, the medium re-review took 39 s against 79 s for the high first review in the same run. It confirmed the fix and found nothing new.

- **Spend:** −$0.46 a run (−43%). The plan turn is gone (−$0.36). The Critic loop fell from $0.32 to $0.22: fewer rounds because there are no plan-turn sub-steps to enforce, and a cheaper re-review. The Builder did not need to read more without Spark's plan ($0.38 against $0.35).

The same-run comparison is the fair one: arm a now takes 1.6× direct Claude's wall time and 1.6× its cost per pass.
- **Pass rate:** unchanged, 100% in both (n = 2; the story is easy). Nothing to revert.

What still separates arm a from direct Claude: an Opus Builder where direct uses Sonnet ($0.38 vs $0.37 for the whole direct job), plus a $0.14–0.22 Critic and its 75–120 s.

## Limits

- **n = 2 after, on one easy story.** Every arm passes it, so this shows cost and time, not pass-rate safety. Change 1 needs watching on real spec-like requests, especially Builders that stop after the first slice (the increment rule). Neither re-run Builder did.
- **Load confounds wall time.** The original arm-a runs ran at load averages of 16–300; the re-run ran at 7–42. Same-session direct Claude (116 s against 141 s before) suggests load explains part of the drop, and the Critic's first review also sped up (75–79 s against 125–184 s). The spend and answer changes don't depend on load.
- **Codex costs are estimates** from token counts at card prices. They agree with Spark's recorded Critic costs.
- **The medium re-review ran once** (a-2). Its speed-up (39 s against 79 s) is one observation.
- **Only Automatic was measured.** At Full auto, change 1 also removes the plan card's countdown. At Review each step nothing changes.
